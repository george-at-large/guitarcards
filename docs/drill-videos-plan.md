# Guitar drill videos: plan v4

14 Nov 2026. The complete series. It replaces v3 and keeps v3's format, which the pilots settled: **content, not
practice**. Each video shows a drill's complete set in G across the whole neck, talks only to name the drill, and
shows extras as on-screen text. Videos are built with `tools/drillvideo` (see its README); the chord, turnaround
and scale-family videos are generated by `gen.py`.

Status: **made** = rendered and sent; **planned** = specified here, not built.

---

## 1. The format

| | Rule |
|---|---|
| The drill | A silent card straight after the title states the exercise as 2–4 numbered steps, exactly as you'll practise it. Nothing plays until the drill is stated. |
| Both ways | Where a technique is optional (an anticipation, a substitution, a sound), the video drills it three ways: without it, with it every time, then by choice in some places. |
| Length | Whatever the content needs, usually 2–5 min; over 6 min splits into a/b. |
| Key | G. It ends with a 12-key table, or the rule for moving to the next key. |
| Voice | Only to name the drill and each section: about 200–600 characters per video. No captions. |
| Neck | The whole neck (frets 0–17) unless the drill lives in one region. Each shape is played once. |
| Labels | Every dot shows its interval. The big line names the shape; the line under it says where it is or what moved. |
| Maps and morphs | Ladders end on a whole-neck map. Qualities that are a one-note change of another are shown as morphs, with the moving note ringed. |
| Grooves | Genre, strumming and picking videos show a right-hand grid under the neck: the count, thumb and fingers (p, ima), strums ↓ ↑, muted strokes ×↓ ×↑, picks and slurs, with the sounding slot lit. |
| Extras | Watch-outs, rules of thumb and Marbin's ideas appear as silent text, at most two lines. |
| Ending | 12 keys (or roots), how much to do a day, then "You've got it when". |

## 2. Best practice

The habits are taught in Video 0 and the on-screen notes, and built into how each drill is stated.

| Principle | In the videos |
|---|---|
| **Learn in one key, then mix.** Practising one thing at a time ("blocked") feels faster, but mixing items ("interleaved", random order) gives better retention in motor learning. | Each video teaches in G; the 12-key tables and daily-dose notes then mix keys, regions and string sets. |
| **Retrieve, don't read.** Effortful recall ("desirable difficulties") beats re-reading. | Each drill says to name the bass, degree or next chord before playing it; the next item shows a beat early. |
| **Vary the conditions.** Practising in varied conditions transfers better. | Every drill runs over keys, areas, string sets or paths, not one fixed version. |
| **Short and spaced.** Spacing helps motor learning on average, but one piano study found no effect, so the evidence for complex instrumental skills is mixed. | The daily dose stays at about 10 minutes per drill, revisiting an old key each day. |
| **Clean before fast.** | Tempo-ladder notes: add 4 BPM per clean pass, drop 10 when it breaks. |
| **Hear it.** | Drones under modes and scales, so each colour note is heard against its root. |

The evidence is mostly from general motor-learning and verbal-learning research; there are few controlled studies on
guitarists. Sources:
- [Interleaving for instrument practice](https://www.retrievalpractice.org/strategies/2019/4/3/interleaving)
- [Spacing and motor learning in piano (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5553926)
- [Kirk, deliberate practice and desirable difficulties (DMA thesis)](https://d1rbsgppyrdqq4.cloudfront.net/s3fs-public/c7/Kirk_asu_0010E_24069.pdf)

### 3.7b Fingerpicking and Travis picking

Taught the way the strumming videos are: each pattern gets a name, its grid, one slow bar and two bars at tempo, about
11 s each. Combinations take about 20 s each (two slow bars, then two passes of two bars at tempo). The grid marks
which finger plays which string: p on 6, 5 or 4; i, m and a on 3, 2 and 1; pinches as p+m. Muted thumb notes show
as ×p.

The research agrees on the core: a steady alternating thumb is the foundation, the fingers add melody or arpeggios
above it, and the standard variations are pinches, rolls, reversed bass order, half-bar fragments, walking or
descending bass, and syncopated melody between the thumb notes. Chet Atkins built most of his playing from
alternating bass plus syncopated melody. Sources:
- [JG Music Lessons, Travis picking](https://jgmusiclessons.com/how-to-play-travis-picking-patterns-on-guitar-with-audio-and-tabs)
- [JG Music Lessons, fingerpicking patterns](https://jgmusiclessons.com/fingerpicking-patterns/)
- [Dummies, Travis picking](https://www.dummies.com/article/academics-the-arts/music/instruments/guitar/how-to-play-travis-picking-on-guitar-198150)
- [MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/how-to/guitar-lesson-how-to-start-travis-picking-your-chords)
- [Premier Guitar, Chet Atkins primer](https://www.premierguitar.com/lessons/one-note-at-a-time-a-chet-atkins-primer)
- [TrueFire, rolls](https://learn.truefire.com/fingerpicking-patterns-for-guitar)

### 3.9 Repertoire excerpts across genres

About 3 minutes each, longer and harder than the drill videos. An excerpt is one or two phrases, a section, or a
famous passage, enough to own the piece's central idea. Each one is arranged here for guitar, so its tab is always
available. Difficulty runs from 1 (early intermediate) to 5 (advanced).

**Format of an excerpt video:**
1. The drill card: what to learn, in steps.
2. The excerpt at tempo once, with the tab scrolling under the neck.
3. Phrase by phrase, slowly. Each phrase has a short analysis panel: its chord and Roman numeral, the device in use
   (pedal, sequence, suspension, borrowed chord…), and a fingering note. More text is fine wherever it's informative.
4. The device isolated as a short drill, for example the Andalusian cadence in four positions.
5. At tempo again, then "you've got it when".

Voice only where it adds something the screen doesn't; often none.

**Rights:**
- **PD:** public domain everywhere.
- **life+70:** public domain in the EU, UK, Brazil and similar, but not yet in the US. (India is life+60, so these are public domain there too.)
- **US:** public domain in the US only (published 1930 or earlier).

For traditional forms, the falsetas, guajeos and phrases are written for the course. Copyrighted songs (the cantos,
Jobim, Django's US releases…) are taught as harmony and right hand only, never their melodies.
