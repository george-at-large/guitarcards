# Audiation curriculum

A plan for guided audiation: hearing chords, their tones and the way voices move between them, in the mind. It is built
from research on audiation, auditory imagery, perceptual learning and aural-skills teaching. Tags: **[E]** research
evidence, **[P]** practitioner method, **[I]** our inference. The direct evidence on ear training is thin: most [E] rules are
carried over from general category learning or auditory perceptual learning. Sources are listed at the end.

## Seven rules everything follows

1. **Hear it before you imagine it.** A tone is always sounded and isolated before you are asked to hold or restore it.
   Silent gaps trigger imagery only for material already familiar (Kraemer 2005) [E]. Gordon puts discrimination (echo
   what was heard) before inference (produce the unheard) [P]. Predicting an *unheard* tone comes later, as a separate
   step.
2. **Whole → part → whole.** Every cycle starts and ends with the full chord [P, Gordon].
3. **Generate, then check.** Every act of imagination is followed by the sounding answer, after a short silence. Failing
   to retrieve still helps, but only when feedback follows (Kornell 2009) [E]. The answer is never played before you
   have tried.
4. **Keep imagery windows clean.** You imagine either in silence or over the chord *with the target removed*, never while
   new pitches sound (Deutsch, interference in pitch memory) [E]. Narration is fine.
5. **Imagine at most three notes at once, building up from one.** Errors climb sharply from three voices of one timbre
   (Huron 1989), and working memory holds about 2–3 tones (Li et al. 2013) [E]. For a four-note chord, imagine three
   over a sounding root.
6. **Remove support gradually.** Start with the target louder and lit, then at normal level, then muted but lit, then
   muted and unlit. Vanishing cues (Glisky 1986), backward fading (Renkl 2002), easy before hard (Ahissar &
   Hochstein 1997) [E, carried over from other domains].
7. **Name functions, not letters.** Say "the third", "the six-minor chord", "the bass falls a third"; the letter name
   shows on screen. This keeps the skill relative and lets keys rotate [I].

## The core cycle: one tone, about 40 seconds

The building block of every lesson. The example is the 3rd of a major-seventh chord.

| # | What sounds | Neck | Narrator | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | the full chord | all lit | "The chord." | whole |
| 2 | the 3rd alone | 3rd lit | "This is the third." | isolate |
| 3 | the chord, 3rd boosted 6 dB | all lit, 3rd bright | "Find it inside the chord." | hear it out, with support |
| 4 | the chord without the 3rd | 3rd as an empty ring | "The third is gone. Notice the hole." | remove |
| 5 | the same gap chord | ring | "Put it back, in your head." (3 s) | restore in the mind |
| 6 | the 3rd comes back | lit | "Check." | feedback |
| 7 | the chord without the 3rd | ring | "Once more, on your own." (3 s) | faded repeat |
| 8 | the full chord | all lit | — | whole |

Faded versions in later passes:
- step 3 at normal level;
- step 5 with the ring unlit;
- step 5 in full silence ("hold it with nothing sounding").

## Other exercise types (built from the cycle)

- **Stack and peel.**
  - Tones enter in tuning order: root, 5th, 3rd, 7th [P, ensemble tuning].
  - They leave in reverse order.
  - Last round: a tone leaves silently and you're asked "which one left?", with a pause before the answer.
- **Minimal pair.** Two chords that differ by one tone, e.g. C and Cm, or Cmaj7 and C7.
  - Isolate the tone that changes.
  - Then play the chord without it: "imagine major… now minor", checking each one.
  - Chord types are taught as pairs like this [P, Gordon's contrast; E, studies on triads that differ only in the third].
- **Top-voice migration.**
  - Hear the tone on top, where it is easiest to pick out [E, high-voice superiority].
  - Re-voice the chord so the same tone sits in the middle, find it again, then remove it and restore it there.
- **Common-tone thread.**
  - Move from chord A to chord B with the shared tone soloed through the change.
  - Repeat without the solo, and ask "which note stayed?" with a pause before the answer.
- **Guide-tone thread.**
  - In ii–V–I: the 7th of ii falls a half step to the 3rd of V, then holds as the 7th of I.
  - The line alone → inside the chords, lit → chords without the line while you imagine it → check [P, Galper, Ligon; E, Huron 2001 on stepwise lines being easy to follow].
- **Outer voices, then the middle by subtraction.**
  - Bass alone, then top voice alone, then full chords.
  - Ask "what's in the middle?", then solo the middle voice as the check [E/P, Chenette 2021].
- **Bass ladder** for progressions, removing support step by step:
  1. full chords;
  2. bass plus guide tones;
  3. bass alone;
  4. imagine the bass too.
- **Predict the unheard** (only once a tone type is familiar). On a new root: "imagine its seventh before it sounds", then check [E, Brod 2018].
- **Plain listening.** 20–30 seconds of the day's chords with no task at all. Listening alongside practice made interval learning generalise (Little, Cheng & Wright 2019) [E].

## Session anatomy (6–8 minutes, daily)

1. **Orient** (10 s). Today's key: a short cadence or the tonic chord [P].
2. **Review** (1–1.5 min). Mixed items from earlier levels, weighted to what you recently missed, each one predict-then-check [E, successive relearning].
3. **New material, blocked** (3–4 min). Core cycles and one other exercise type, with support fading across passes.
4. **Plain listening** (30 s).
5. **Interleave** (1 min). The new item mixed with its confusable neighbours [E, Carvalho & Goldstone 2014; Brunmair & Richter 2019].
6. **Close** (20 s). Play it on the guitar and compare [E, Steenstrup 2021: imagery plus playing helped].

Sessions stay short and frequent: 8-minute sessions beat hour-long ones for early learning (Molloy 2012), and learning
consolidates between days and overnight [E]. A new chord type gets one or two blocked sessions, then joins the
interleaved review [E/I].

**Daily dose.** One session alone may be below the amount needed to consolidate (Wright & Sabin 2007) [E]. If we build
the app, pair each session with about 10 minutes of the app's chord test on the same categories. The test adapts to keep
you near 80–85% correct (Wilson 2019) [E, a theoretical result].

**Advancing.** Move up a level on 85–90% in a mixed test, in keys you haven't practised, on two separate days. Learners
overrate blocked practice, and in-session scores overstate learning (Wong 2021) [E/I].

## The levels

Orders follow the research: for tones, top voice → bass → 3rd → 7th → 5th, the 5th last because it fuses with the root
(Palmer & Holleran 1994; Chenette 2021) [E/I]. Chord types come in one-tone-different pairs. Progressions build from
common tones → half-step motion → guide-tone pairs → root motion → outer voices → inner voices [E/P/I].
**Keys rotate from level 1** [I, from high-variability training].

### Stage 1 · Tones inside a triad (5 sessions)
- **1.1 Major triad.** Core cycle on the top voice, the bass, then the 3rd.
- **1.2 Minor triad.** The major vs minor pair: only the 3rd moves.
- **1.3 The 5th.** Stack and peel (root, 5, 3); "which one left?".
- **1.4 Inversions.** The same triad with the 3rd on top, then in the middle (top-voice migration).
- **1.5 Colours of the triad.** Diminished and augmented (the 5th moves); sus2 and sus4 (the 3rd replaced). Then review.

### Stage 2 · Seventh chords (6 sessions)
- **2.1 maj7.** The 7th as the top voice, then in the middle. Imagine three tones over the sounding root.
- **2.2 maj7 vs 7.** Only the 7th moves.
- **2.3 7 vs m7.** Only the 3rd moves.
- **2.4 m7 vs m7♭5.** Only the 5th moves.
- **2.5 dim7 and m(maj7).**
- **2.6 Guitar shells.** 1–3–7 and 1–7–3 voicings of all five types; interleaved review.

### Stage 3 · Two chords: what moves, what stays (5 sessions)
- **3.1 Common tones.** I–vi (Cmaj7 → Am7): two tones stay, the bass falls, 7 rises to 1. This is the current pilot, rebuilt on the core cycle.
- **3.2 One voice by a half step.** I → I7 → IV: the root slides down to ♭7, then that ♭7 becomes the 3rd of IV.
- **3.3 Guide tones.** V7 → I: 7 falls to 3, 3 holds as 7.
- **3.4 Root motion by fifths.** The bass alone, then bass plus guide tones.
- **3.5 Review.** Mixed two-chord moves, in new keys.

### Stage 4 · ii–V–I as a single unit (5 sessions)
- **4.1 The guide-tone thread** through major ii–V–I.
- **4.2 Outer voices,** then the inner voices by subtraction.
- **4.3 Minor iiø–V7♭9–i:** what changes against major.
- **4.4 The bass ladder** on ii–V–I, ending with the bass imagined too.
- **4.5 Mixed major and minor ii–V–I** in rotating keys.

### Stage 5 · Diatonic progressions (5 sessions)
- **5.1** I–vi–ii–V.
- **5.2** I–IV–V and the cadences (authentic, plagal, half, deceptive).
- **5.3** iii–vi–ii–V.
- **5.4** Four-chord loops (the pop axis): hear the bass line, then which chord is minor.
- **5.5** Progression review using the bass ladder.

These line up with Ear Gym levels g1–g7: the app tests what the sessions teach.

### Stage 6 · Colour and chromatic harmony (8 sessions)
- **6.1 9ths and 13ths** over maj7, m7 and 7. Each tension is isolated, then restored over a sounding shell.
- **6.2 Altered tensions on the dominant:** ♭9, ♯9, ♯11, ♭13, taught as pairs (9 vs ♭9, 13 vs ♭13).
- **6.3 Borrowed chords:** iv, ♭VI, ♭VII. The tone that darkens is isolated against its diatonic neighbour (iv vs IV: the 6th becomes ♭6).
- **6.4 Secondary dominants:** VI7 vs vi7 (one tone raised).
- **6.5 Tritone substitution:** ii–♭II7–I. The bass slides by half steps; the guide tones are shared with V7.
- **6.6 Backdoor:** iv–♭VII7–I.
- **6.7 Passing diminished:** I–♯I°7–ii.
- **6.8 Review.**

These line up with Ear Gym levels g8–g12.

### Stage 7 · Creating (open-ended)
- Imagine a voicing of your own, then play it.
- Imagine a progression's bass line, then sing it, then play it.
- Hear a tune's changes from its bass and guide tones.
- This is Gordon's last stage (creativity) [P].

Altogether: about 34 sessions plus Stage 7. At one a day, with review days, that's two to three months.

## What the builder needs for this

The current step types (`sound`, `drop`, `stop`, `move`, `hear`, `imagine`, `clear`) cover most of the plan. Still to add:
- **`solo`:** play one voice alone while the others stay silent but keep their rings.
- **`boost`:** a voice louder by N dB, for the first rung of the fading ladder.
- **`ask` / `pause`:** a question, a silence for your answer, then the reveal. The answer is said aloud, and in the app, tapped.
- **`orient`:** a short cadence in the session's key.
- **Templates:** each exercise type above (`cycle: {tone, fade}`, `pair`, `peel`, `thread`, `ladder`), so a session is
  written as a list of exercises rather than a list of steps.
- **Keys:** a `key` field, with voicings given as interval shapes relative to the root, so the same session renders in any key.
